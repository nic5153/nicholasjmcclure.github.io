How targets are judged, and data sources

Can the rig capture it?

For every event the planner builds an exposure plan for your camera and predicts the photometric scatter, then compares it with the size of the signal:

  • Saturation. Exposure is chosen up to the cadence the event needs; if the star would exceed 70% of full well it is defocused (up to the limit in Setup) and then the exposure is shortened. A star that still saturates at the minimum exposure is rejected as too bright.
  • Noise. Star, sky, read and dark noise per frame plus scintillation (Young 1967 with the Osborn et al. 2015 correction), and the noise of a three-star comparison ensemble drawn from the expected star counts in your field. A correlated "systematics" floor is added in quadrature.
  • Sky. Site sky brightness (Bortle or SQM), airmass brightening, twilight, and moonlight using Krisciunas & Schaefer (1991) at the event time.
  • S/N. Transits: depth divided by the uncertainty of the in-transit minus out-of-transit level. Eclipses and pulsators: amplitude divided by the scatter of a bin sized to the event. Go ≥ the Go threshold, Marginal ≥ the Marginal threshold.

Can the site see it?

  • The whole required window (transit plus baselines, or the eclipse minimum, or a full pulsation cycle) must lie in darkness (Sun below your limit) with the target above your minimum altitude and horizon mask.
  • Transit windows are padded by the propagated ephemeris uncertainty (up to 45 min). Predictions convert BJDTDB / HJD to UTC including the light-time correction.
  • Eclipse durations come from VSX; where VSX has none, a period-based estimate is used and flagged.

Sources

  • Transiting planets: ExoClock ephemerides (Kokori et al. 2023) and the NASA Exoplanet Archive.
  • Variable stars: the AAVSO International Variable Star Index (VSX).
