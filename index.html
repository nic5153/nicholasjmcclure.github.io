About

I am an astrophysics research assistant at Texas Tech University with a B.S. in physics (astrophysics) and a minor in mathematics, with a passion for time-domain astronomy, cataclysmic variables, and stellar pulsations. I work primarily with large data sets such as TESS, ZTF, and ground-based observations to investigate periodic variability in compact binary systems and pulsating stars.

My core interests lie in stellar structure and evolution, accretion physics, and asteroseismology, with an emphasis on signal processing. I am also passionate about public and academic outreach and science education. My research experience has given me a strong foundation in astronomical image calibration, observational strategy and planning, public communication, and extensive Python work with NumPy, SciPy, Astropy, Tkinter, Matplotlib and more. My goal is to continue this work by earning a PhD in Astronomy/Astrophysics.

Outside of my academic pursuits, I enjoy astrophotography, orchestral and wind ensemble music, and spending time with my dog, Orion, and my cat, Cassie (short for Cassiopeia).

Research interests

  • Time-domain astronomy and photometric variability in compact binary systems
  • Accretion physics and outbursts in cataclysmic variables
  • Time-series analysis of large data sets
  • Asteroseismology and stellar evolution
