How objects are judged, and data sources

Worth imaging tonight?

  • Time. The object must stay above your minimum altitude and horizon mask for at least the minimum time on target while the Sun is below the imaging limit (astronomical darkness by default).
  • Framing. It should fit within 85% of the sensor's field (rotate the camera to match the long axis). Larger objects are marked as mosaics; very small ones are flagged.
  • Signal. Integration is the luminance time to reach S/N 30 per 2″×2″ element at the object's mean surface brightness, using your sky brightness, airmass and the Moon (Krisciunas & Schaefer 1991) at the best time of night. Galaxy surface brightness comes from OpenNGC; nebulae are floored at 21 mag/arcsec² because catalogue magnitudes often include embedded stars. Objects beyond the integration limit in Setup are rejected; the same object may become worthwhile on a moonless night.

Exposures

  • Sub-exposures are sky-limited: long enough that sky noise swamps read noise (sky ≥ swamp factor × RN²), capped by your guiding limit. RGB subs are longer than luminance because each colour filter passes about a third of the sky light.
  • Tonight's time is split roughly half luminance and half RGB; dither every few frames.

Sources

  • Objects: OpenNGC by Mattia Verga (CC BY-SA 4.0): all Messier objects plus NGC/IC objects brighter than about magnitude 12.
  • Framing images: DSS2 colour via CDS hips2fits. Weather: Open-Meteo.
